Dispatch, transformer currents and declared ratings

Generator dispatch and voltage-bound applicability

A reported dispatch inside a power box is not enough: recompute power using the correct connection voltage and supplied current. The local candidate companion now includes a package-owned 10 V example with a $10+2\mathrm j$ VA load, $4+1\mathrm j$ VA generator injection and $6+1\mathrm j$ VA source injection. The currents are respectively $1-0.2\mathrm j$, $0.4-0.1\mathrm j$ and $0.6-0.1\mathrm j$ A. Their signs and explicit neutral returns close KCL. Equal lower/upper generator P/Q bounds fix the native comparison point; this example does not claim to optimize dispatch or generation cost.

Predict. If a result reports 3 W but voltage and current imply 4 W, can a 3 W upper bound be certified? No. The independent evaluation detects both the 1 W report discrepancy and the 1 W bound violation. The report is evidence to check, not a substitute for the defining power relation.

The phase and return current magnitudes are $\sqrt{0.17}\approx 0.4123$ A. A 0.5 A phase cap passes while a 0.4 A return cap fails. With multiple WYE phases the return magnitude is $|\sum_k I_k|$, not $\sum_k|I_k|$ and not any individual phase magnitude. For example, two in-phase 1 A injections give a 2 A return; opposite injections cancel. At zero coil voltage a power identity can still hide nonzero current, so current limits and KCL remain separate checks.

For the finite native WYE/SINGLE_PHASE domain, generator power uses the explicitly mapped coil voltage. Omitting the neutral from a WYE generator map does not borrow the bus neutral. A two-terminal SINGLE_PHASE generator uses its ordered pair, which may also be phase-to-phase. The later DELTA-generator section extends this scope: the load's two-terminal DELTA convention must not be copied into another device family without checking that family's executable contract.

Bus-voltage limits ask a different coordinate question. The supported v_min/v_max arrays bound phase-to-earth magnitude in non-neutral terminal declaration order. Moving the neutral changes a phase-neutral generator power without changing the phase's earth voltage. Tests use such a perturbed candidate to distinguish those observations; the separate switch-voltage check rejects that candidate. An unchanged bus-bound result is not a certificate for it.

The native builder skips ordinary bus bounds at source-fixed and perfectly grounded terminals. The candidate records those bound obligations as unassessed, even if their declarations happen to agree with the source. For the 10 V source and 9 V upper bound, a native source equation does not enforce the declared bound. This slice does not silently label it passed, reinterpret the bound as phase-to-neutral, or remove its array index. Other bound families, nonfinite/malformed arrays, malformed generator prices/controllers and unsupported transformer checks retain explicit coverage limits. Known current contributions remain usable when only limit/report evidence is missing; missing currents cannot be filled in from power by dividing by voltage.

Transformer series currents are not all terminal currents

The next local candidate slice covers one explicit terminal pair on each side of the native single_phase transformer, with fixed tap, finite leakage and secondary excitation. It complements the transformer-port lesson by checking supplied native current observations as well as equations. It does not by itself cover the running network's n_winding device; the later winding-list and rating-metadata slices supply that distinct coverage.

Let $U_f,U_t$ be oriented coil voltages and $I_f,I_t$ the supplied series currents, both positive into their winding. For this native tap convention,

\[N_0=V_{\mathrm{nom},f}/V_{\mathrm{nom},t},\qquad N=N_0\tau,\qquad Z'=Z_t+Z_f/N_0^2,\]

\[U_f-NU_t=N^2Z'I_f,\qquad NI_f+I_t=0.\]

The fixed multiplier $\tau$ changes the effective ratio and the from-referred leakage $N^2Z'$. Replacing that leakage with $Z_f+N^2Z_t$ agrees at $\tau=1$ but generally differs away from it. This is a declared parameterization of the implemented equivalent circuit, not a universal law for every transformer tap or leakage model. Zero leakage is handled directly as an ideal voltage relation; no small impedance is inserted.

The excitation admittance $Y$ lies across the secondary coil. Consequently,

\[I_{t,\mathrm{terminal}}=I_t+YU_t,\qquad S_{\mathrm{terminal}}=U_f\overline{I_f} +U_t\overline{I_t+YU_t} =Z'|I_t|^2+\overline Y|U_t|^2.\]

The last equality follows from the voltage and current relations and is checked separately. The signed terminal returns enter KCL. The excitation current is reconstructed from $Y,U_t$; this is not an independent excitation measurement. Native fr/1, to/1 currents and their cm values refer to the series currents, while i_max_to bounds the secondary terminal current. The positive nameplate s_rating bounds from-coil apparent power.

Predict. Does a reported secondary cm above i_max_to necessarily violate the implemented terminal-current limit? It does not: the two fields can describe different observations when excitation is present.

Coupled winding leakage and the observation being rated

The preceding two-port example does not determine the rating semantics of a winding-list model. The next local candidate checks two or three WYE windings with one phase-neutral coil each, fixed nominal ratios, explicit pairwise short-circuit reactances, and excitation on winding 2. It uses the native n_winding equations, independently evaluated in SI units. This smaller witness does not by itself assess the running network's multiphase arrangement; the later slices address incidence and rating metadata separately. Other unsupported declarations remain explicit obligations.

Let $U_k$ denote phase-minus-neutral coil voltage and $I_k$ bare coil current, positive into the transformer at every winding. Define

\[N_k=V_{\mathrm{nom},k}/V_{\mathrm{nom},1},\qquad j_k=N_k I_k,\qquad R_k=r_k/N_k^2.\]

Here $r_k$ is in ohms on winding $k$'s own voltage base; the supplied pairwise $X_{ab}$ values are already referred to winding 1. With $Z_{ab}=R_a+R_b+\mathrm jX_{ab}$, form the coupled matrix

\[(Z_B)_{ii}=Z_{1,i+1},\qquad (Z_B)_{ij}=\tfrac12(Z_{1,i+1}+Z_{1,j+1}-Z_{i+1,j+1})\quad(i\ne j).\]

The current and voltage equations are

\[\sum_{k=1}^{n}j_k=0,\qquad U_1-U_k/N_k=-(Z_Bj_{2:n})_{k-1}\quad(k=2,\ldots,n).\]

The minus sign follows from using inward currents on all ports. For two windings, $j_2=-I_1$ turns it into the familiar positive source-current voltage drop. For three windings, the nonreference currents are coupled through the off-diagonal term. Replacing this matrix by independent two-port branches generally changes the equations. Reordering windings requires reordering their pairwise data, ratios and result labels; excitation attached to winding 2 also makes its position part of the declared model.

The package fixture chooses $N=(1,1/2,1/4)$, $r=(1/4,1/16,1/64)$ Ω and $(X_{12},X_{13},X_{23})=(1/2,3/4,3/4)$ Ω, giving

\[Z_B=\begin{bmatrix} 1/2+\mathrm j/2 & 1/4+\mathrm j/4\\ 1/4+\mathrm j/4 & 1/2+3\mathrm j/4 \end{bmatrix}\ \Omega.\]

An adequate winding total can hide an overloaded phase

The next local candidate extends the winding-list audit to matched one-, two-, and three-phase WYE windings. This is an extension of a specific executable model: each winding has an explicit neutral, and the coil counts agree. The native model pairs coils by position in their phase lists, after removing the neutral. A terminal label identifies a local terminal; it does not automatically define a cross-winding phase correspondence.

For coil position $p$, the same current and voltage equations hold separately:

\[\sum_k N_k I_{kp}=0,\qquad U_{1p}-U_{kp}/N_k=-(Z_Bj_{2:n,p})_{k-1}.\]

The model does not add mutual leakage between different phase positions. Excitation acts across each winding-2 coil. Its phase terminal currents and neutral return, oriented out of the bus into the transformer, are

\[T_{kp}=I_{kp}+\mathbf{1}_{k=2}YU_{2p},\qquad T_{k,n}=-\sum_{p=1}^{m}T_{kp}.\]

Losses sum the preceding per-coil identities over $p$. The neutral is the negative complex sum, not the sum of magnitudes. Opposite current errors in two phases can leave this neutral sum unchanged while violating their individual ampere-turn equations and local phase KCL. Likewise, a winding neutral current is not an earth-reaction measurement. The computational fixture has nonzero neutral currents and separately supplied zero earth reactions.

Predict. A winding has a 12 VA rating, with three coil powers whose magnitudes sum to less than 12 VA. Is it necessarily compliant with the native winding-list limit? No: this implementation allocates equal shares,

\[|U_{kp}\overline{I_{kp}}|\le S_{k,\max}/m\quad\text{for every }p.\]

That constraint bounds each bare coil before excitation is added to its terminal current. It differs from either aggregate constraint $|\sum_p U_{kp}\overline{I_{kp}}|\le S_{k,\max}$ or $\sum_p|U_{kp}\overline{I_{kp}}|\le S_{k,\max}$. Satisfying every allocated share implies both aggregate bounds; the converse fails in general. This allocation rule describes the implemented limit, not a universal thermal-rating rule for all physical transformers.

DELTA line currents cannot reveal internal circulation

The coil-incidence lesson distinguishes a coil current from its terminal observation. The next local package candidate applies that distinction to two/three-winding transformers. A supported DELTA winding has exactly three distinct phase terminals and no neutral in its map; other devices at the same bus may still use a neutral. Its ordered phases and rotation define the directed coil pairs. For phase order $(a,b,c)$, rotation $+1$ gives $(a,b),(b,c),(c,a)$; rotation $-1$ gives $(a,c),(b,a),(c,b)$. These are conventions relative to the declared order, not standalone vector-group labels.

Let $D$ have one row per coil, with +1 at its first terminal and -1 at its second. The voltage/current pairing is

\[U=Dv,\qquad I_{\mathrm{port}}=I_{\mathrm{coil}}+I_{\mathrm{exc}},\qquad I_{\mathrm{line}}=D^{\mathsf T}I_{\mathrm{port}},\qquad v^{\mathsf T}\overline{I_{\mathrm{line}}} =U^{\mathsf T}\overline{I_{\mathrm{port}}}.\]

Excitation is present only on winding 2 in this native model. The independent audit enumerates the pairs directly, then evaluates the same per-coil leakage and ampere-turn equations as before. It reconstructs bus line currents with the power-dual incidence and marks them reconstructed, without a residual or an independent-measurement claim. WYE neutral-return rules do not apply to DELTA.

Predict: if the bare coil current is $0.5-0.25\mathrm j$ A but the line current at a is $0.875-0.375\mathrm j$ A, does a native 0.6 A winding i_max pass? In this implementation it does: that limit bounds each bare coil, whose magnitude is approximately 0.559 A, rather than the approximately 0.952 A line current. The package-owned delta-winding.json fixture supplies this finite-leakage DELTA/WYE point, with rotation -1, winding-2 excitation, explicit internal currents and SI voltage/current reports. A native solve agrees with the analytic point and passes the independent audit. The scalar s_max/3 allocation uses actual line-to-line coil voltage for DELTA.

A retained rating field may declare no loading limit

The per-coil allocation example required an explicit loading constraint. A rating field can instead serve as an impedance conversion base, a retained nameplate, or a native loading cap. Its name and presence alone do not establish which role reaches the solved model.

For the native n_winding path reviewed here, top-level and per-winding s_rating are power-base/nameplate metadata. Each winding's optional s_max separately declares the total apparent-power cap, allocated equally among its coils. This differs from the earlier single_phase recipe, whose s_rating actually creates a from-coil loading constraint. A package-wide "all transformer nameplates are enforced" inference is therefore unsafe.

The conversion trace also matters. PowerIO 0.11.6 converts source kVA into winding rating metadata. Its BMOPF schema-0.1.0 emission retains the winding-list record under extras; BMOPFTools restores it during normalization and preserves the ratings without inventing s_max. Conversion uses rating bases to turn percentages into ohms, with a package-owned correction for the imported winding resistance convention. Once the supplied SI coefficients are fixed, editing rating metadata alone does not rederive their ohmic values. The direct SI audit evaluates those coefficients; it does not authenticate the earlier conversion or infer an omitted equipment limit.

A generator price is not an objective certificate

A generator price declaration has a different role from an electrical limit. In the bounded native generator form it is a full finite signed vector, one coefficient per declared coil. A two-terminal SINGLE_PHASE device has one coil price, including when its terminal order is reversed. A WYE neutral has no separate price. Scalars, polynomial-shaped objects, wrong or extra lengths, nulls, Booleans and unrepresentable values remain unassessed rather than being reinterpreted as another cost model.

The native default generator contribution is

\[C_g=\sum_k c_{g,k}\frac{P^{\mathrm{inj}}_{g,k}}{1000},\qquad P^{\mathrm{inj}}_{g,k}=\Re\left(U_{g,k}\overline{I_{g,k}}\right).\]

With power in W and price in currency/kWh, $C_g$ is a rate in currency/hour. It is linear in active power, although bilinear in IVR voltage/current variables. A positive coefficient penalizes its injection contribution; a negative one rewards it within the selected minimization objective. Other terms and network constraints can alter actual dispatch. Zero direct cost alone establishes neither a feasible flat direction nor dispatch non-uniqueness.

The native cost-expression constructor adds no variables or constraints. Generator apparent-power limits already create auxiliaries independently of price. Per-unit working prices are multiplied by the local power base to cancel power scaling; this is coordinate bookkeeping, not a changed tariff.
